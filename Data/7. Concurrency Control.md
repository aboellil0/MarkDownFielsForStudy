
## 🔐 1. **Pessimistic Concurrency Control** (Lock First, Then Act)

### 🧠 Idea:

> "Assume conflict will happen — **lock the data** so no one else can change it while I work."

---

### 📦 Example:

You’re editing a shared Google Doc, and you **lock a section** so others can’t change it until you’re done.

---

### 🔒 How it works:

- When a transaction reads or writes data, it **locks** it.
    
- Other transactions must **wait** for the lock to release.
    
- Prevents conflicts like **dirty reads, lost updates**, etc.
    

---

### ✅ Good for:

- High-conflict environments (lots of writes)
    
- Scenarios where **waiting is better than errors**
    

---

## 🌀 2. **Two-Phase Locking (2PL)**

### 🧠 Idea:

> “Get all the locks you need first, then release them all at once.”

---

### 🔄 Phases:

1. **Growing phase** → Acquire locks, but don’t release any.
    
2. **Shrinking phase** → Release locks, but don’t acquire any more.
    

---

### 🔐 Benefit:

- Guarantees **serializability** (safe, ordered transactions).
    
- Ensures no other transaction sees half-done work.
    

---

### ⚠️ Downside:

- Can cause **deadlocks** (two transactions waiting forever).
    

---

## 🟢 3. **Optimistic Concurrency Control** (Act First, Check Later)

### 🧠 Idea:

> "Assume there’s no conflict — but before saving, **check if the data changed**. If it did, cancel or retry."

---

### 📦 Example:

You and your friend are editing the same product info. When you hit "Save," the system checks:

- Has it changed since you loaded it?
    
    - If **no**, it updates.
        
    - If **yes**, it **throws a conflict error** or asks you to retry.
        

---

### ✅ Good for:

- Low-conflict environments (many reads, few writes)
    
- Apps that prefer **speed over locking**
    

---

## 📸 4. **Snapshot Isolation** (Work on a Copy)

### 🧠 Idea:

> “Each transaction sees a **frozen snapshot** of the database — changes by others don’t affect you during your transaction.”

---

### 📦 Example:

Imagine reading a book — once you open it, you always see the **same pages** even if the library updates the book later.

---

### 🏃 How it works:

- When a transaction starts, it gets a **snapshot version** of the data.
    
- It doesn’t see changes made by others during its lifetime.
    
- At the end, it checks if anyone updated the same data before committing.
    

---

### ✅ Good for:

- Preventing **non-repeatable reads** and **phantom reads**
    
- Allowing fast reads without locks
    

---

## 🔚 Summary Table

|Technique|Main Idea|Prevents Conflicts?|Performance|Common Use Cases|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Pessimistic Concurrency**|Lock before doing anything|✅ Yes|❌ Slower|High-conflict, critical systems|
|**2-Phase Locking**|Lock → Do work → Unlock (strict)|✅ Yes|⚠️ Can deadlock|Databases ensuring full isolation|
|**Optimistic Concurrency**|Work first, check before saving|✅ Sometimes|✅ Fast|Web APIs, low-conflict systems|
|**Snapshot Isolation**|Work on a frozen view of data|✅ Yes|✅ Efficient|Reporting, multi-read systems|
