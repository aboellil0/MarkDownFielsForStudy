
### 🔍 What Are Data Anomalies?

**Data anomalies** are **problems or inconsistencies** in a database that happen when data is not organized well — especially when it's stored in **one big table** without proper structure.

Imagine you're keeping all your data in one spreadsheet — if it's not designed properly, you might run into strange issues. These issues are called **anomalies**.

---

### 📌 The 3 Main Types of Data Anomalies:

#### 1. **Insertion Anomaly**

- **Problem:** You **can’t add** new data because other data is missing.
    
- **Example:** You want to add a new student, but your table also requires a course name — and the student hasn’t enrolled yet. You’re stuck.
    
- **Real Life:** “I can’t add a new customer until they place an order.”
    

#### 2. **Update Anomaly**

- **Problem:** You update a piece of information in one place, but **forget to update it in other places** — causing **inconsistency**.
    
- **Example:** A customer’s address is stored in 5 places. You update 4, but forget 1. Now the data is inconsistent.
    
- **Real Life:** “Why does this file say my phone number is different?”
    

#### 3. **Deletion Anomaly**

- **Problem:** You delete one piece of data and **accidentally lose important information**.
    
- **Example:** You delete a course because no students are enrolled in it now — but you also lose the course details.
    
- **Real Life:** “We lost all information about a product because no one has bought it recently.”
    

---

### ✅ Why Do These Happen?

Because the data is **not normalized** — meaning:

- Related data is **repeated** a lot.
    
- The structure isn’t **broken into smaller, logical tables** (like separating customers, orders, and products).
    

---

### 🧠 Summary

|Type|Problem|Example|
|---|---|---|
|Insertion|Can’t add data due to missing info|Can’t add a student without enrolling them in a course|

|Deletion|Losing important data accidentally|Deleting an order also deletes customer info|
